# Confidential vesting

Priority: **todo**. App status: **Soon**. The v1 design is accepted; contracts and UI are not implemented yet.

Build recipient-neutral, fully funded confidential token grants with a working treasury creation/funding flow and recipient inspection/claim flow. Use [LlamaPay's fixed vesting flow](https://llamapay.io/vesting) as a product reference. Vesting remains independent from payroll and buyback epochs or budgets, following [ADR 0001](0001-independent-feature-contracts.md).

Use one shared, immutable vesting contract per supported chain for all treasuries and recipients. Start on Sepolia. At contract level, grant creation accepts any compatible Zama ERC-7984 confidential token address, with no token allowlist or deployment-fixed token set. Each grant's token is fixed at creation. The UI offers a configured token list; that list does not restrict direct contract use. Acceptance does not certify a token's implementation: its transfer behavior and any additional restrictions still apply. Grants share custody but have isolated accounting and authorization by grant and token. A grant must never spend another grant's backing. Sharing a contract avoids separate addresses per grant or treasury, but does not conceal relationships exposed by public metadata or transactions.

The wallet creating and funding a grant is its fixed treasury party, authorized to inspect its amounts and revoke it when permitted, and is the refund destination. The recipient is fixed. There are no delegated operators, recipient reassignment, or platform-wide decryption or withdrawal privileges in v1. The contract has no upgrade or platform pause powers; future versions use new deployments while existing grants retain their rules. Multisig decryption and delegated treasury access require a separate design.

Each grant has an allocation, start and end dates, an optional cliff, and a revocability choice fixed at creation. Vesting is continuous and linear from start to end; the cliff gates access to accumulated vesting rather than starting accrual. A four-year grant with a one-year cliff makes 25% available at the first anniversary, then continues vesting linearly. Backdated starts are allowed when the end remains in the future, with an explicit creation preview of immediately vested tokens. The end must follow the start and the optional cliff must fall within the schedule. Allocation and dates cannot be edited, paused, or topped up; additional allocations require new grants. Custom installments and milestones are outside v1.

Fully fund each grant upfront through a confidential token transfer and record the actual amount received as its allocation. A confidential transfer can return zero without reverting: a zero-funded attempt may leave a record, but must not be presented as a funded entitlement. Funding results remain private to the two parties; the UI verifies them through private decryption before showing funding success. A mined transaction alone is insufficient. Retry failed funding by creating a new grant. Funding and payouts remain in confidential tokens; wrapping ordinary tokens is not presented as equivalent privacy because the entering amount can be public.

Only the recipient initiates claims, withdrawing all currently available tokens to the fixed recipient wallet. Claims use the transaction's execution timestamp and reduce outstanding entitlement only by the actual amount transferred. A failed or zero transfer must not erase entitlement. At schedule completion, the full allocation is vested, including any remainder from intermediate rounding.

Revocability is an immutable creation choice, prominently displayed before creation and on the recipient's grant. Revocation stops accrual at its execution timestamp and assigns only unvested tokens to the original treasury wallet. Before the cliff, the entire allocation is unvested; after the cliff, vested but unclaimed tokens remain claimable by the recipient indefinitely. If the refund transfer fails, vesting remains stopped and the treasury can retry withdrawing the outstanding refund. Refund accounting must preserve both the recipient's vested entitlement and other grants' backing.

Recipient and treasury addresses, token identity, schedule dates, and transaction activity are public. Allocation, vested and unvested balances, available amounts, claims, and refunds remain encrypted. Only the grant's treasury party and recipient receive decryption rights over its amounts. Contract permissions needed for encrypted computation do not confer a platform operator's right to inspect plaintext. No treasury receives decryption rights over pooled balances or another grant's data. There is no automatic public disclosure policy.

The UI provides individual grant creation, **Created by me** and **Received by me** views, grant details, private amount inspection, **Claim available**, and revocation/refund retry flows. After authorized decryption, calculate live vesting estimates locally from private amounts and the public schedule, accounting for revocation. Show allocation, vested, available, claimed, and unvested amounts; label live availability as an estimate because execution time determines the claim. Hide amounts before decryption and clear plaintext when the wallet, chain, or feature scope changes, including invalidating pending results. Batch creation and CSV import are outside v1.

Shared custody makes accounting errors consequential across treasuries, so implementation must verify grant isolation, decryption authorization, actual-transfer accounting, cliff boundaries, backdated starts, completion rounding, and revocation/refund retries. The installed OpenZeppelin confidential vesting contracts provide arithmetic and transfer-accounting precedents, but their individual-wallet model is not a drop-in implementation of this shared, fixed-recipient, revocable grant design. This ADR records the accepted behavior, not a claim that implementation or live-chain validation is complete.
